How this is measured

Slot time is slot_duration_median from Trillium: the median time the validator's slots actually took during the epoch, in milliseconds. It is the axis this page sorts on, slowest first, because a validator that is consistently slow is holding up the whole leader rotation.

Avg 15e is the mean of that median over the last 15 epochs, skipping epochs with no record — a single epoch moves around, so the average is the steadier read. Δ is the change from the previous epoch, where a positive number means it got slower. Trend plots the same 15 epochs, newest on the left, and a taller bar means a slower epoch; a gap means no record.

Colours follow the same thresholds throughout: 400 ms or less, up to 420, up to 440, up to 470 and slower than 470. The 400 ms boundary is not arbitrary — it is the allowance IBRL's methodology gives a continuation slot for a perfect slot time score, and the validators split into two clear groups either side of it.

The three score columns come from ibrl.wtf, a community tool from Jito that analyses leader behaviour block by block. They are the components of its IBRL score, kept here as context for why a validator is slow rather than as the ranking itself:

ComponentWeight in IBRLWhat it rewards
Slot score40%Building blocks quickly — a perfect score needs 550ms or less on a handoff slot, 400ms on a continuation slot. Past that the score decays exponentially.
Vote pack15%Processing votes early — a perfect score needs 90% of vote transactions inside the first 48 PoH ticks. Including no votes at all scores 0.
Non-vote45%Spreading work across the block — half from using 50% of compute in the first 32 ticks, half from an even compute distribution across the 64 ticks (Gini coefficient).

Note that IBRL publishes its own median block build time, measured differently from Trillium's slot duration. The two usually agree within a few milliseconds but not always, and this page deliberately shows only Trillium's. Both are kept in the snapshot below if you want to compare them.

Client is what the validator advertises over gossip (getClusterNodes), with the version it reports. Client IDs that have no registered name arrive as Unknown(n) and are resolved with the same table ibrl.wtf uses — that is where Rakurai and Harmonic come from. One case needs more than gossip: running the BAM binary does not by itself make a validator an active BAM validator, so a node advertising BAM is labelled BAM only when it also appears on Jito's BAM roster, and Jito otherwise. Anything still unrecognised is left as its raw Unknown(n) rather than folded into Agave.

This page covers the 369 validators in SFDP Approved state. 358 of them have a slot time for epoch 1010; the remaining 11 have no record for this epoch, so there is nothing to measure and they are left out of the table rather than shown as a zero. Validator names, logos and stake come from the Solana Foundation API. Every source is joined on the identity pubkey and read for the same epoch, so no column is describing a different point in time from its neighbours.

A median taken over a handful of blocks moves a lot epoch to epoch. The ≥ 32 blocks filter drops those rows when you want a steadier read.

The full snapshot behind this page is published as data/ibrl-criteria.json and is versioned in the repository, so any epoch's numbers can be reproduced or diffed later.
